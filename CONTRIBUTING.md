# Contributing to The History of India

Thank you for your interest in contributing to **The History of India** project. This is an open educational initiative dedicated to providing an objective, academically rigorous, and pluralistic account of subcontinental history.

---

## 1. Reporting Errors and Inaccuracies

If you identify an error, anachronism, or typo on any page:

1. **Open an Issue**: Visit our [GitHub Issues](https://github.com/ayushhacksethically-code/india-history/issues) page.
2. **Include the URL**: Specify the exact page file or URL (e.g., `origins.html#urban-exception`).
3. **Cite the Specific Claim**: Quote the exact sentence or statement you believe is inaccurate.
4. **Provide Sources**: Provide academic citations, peer-reviewed papers, archaeological excavation reports, or primary sources supporting the correction.

---

## 2. Suggesting Sources and Historiographical Additions

We welcome recommendations for secondary literature, archaeological reports, and primary source collections:

- When suggesting sources, please include the author, title, publisher/journal, year of publication, and relevant page numbers.
- We prioritize peer-reviewed scholarship, archaeological monographs from the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) and international consortia, epigraphic surveys, and recognized academic presses.

---

## 3. Historical Accuracy is the Paramount Priority

This site is built upon archaeological evidence, epigraphy, numismatics, and peer-reviewed historiography:

- **Plurality over Uniformity**: We document the parallel coexistence of urban, agrarian, pastoral, and forest cultures across all epochs.
- **Evidence-Based Claims**: Speculative timelines, mythological conflations without archaeological corroboration, and revisionist narratives unsupported by material evidence will not be accepted.
- **Academic Neutrality**: Nuanced scholarly debates (such as the linguistic affiliations of IVC or the drying of the Saraswati/Ghaggar-Hakra) are presented with balance and direct citation of evidence.

---

## 4. Code of Conduct

All contributors and commenters are expected to abide by our standards of scholarly conduct:

- **Civil and Respectful Discourse**: Treat fellow contributors, researchers, and maintainers with respect.
- **Strictly Sourced Arguments**: All discussions regarding historical events, dates, figures, and communities must remain grounded in verifiable documentation.
- **No Ideological Grandstanding**: Partisan political agendas, sectarian rhetoric, chauvinism, and bad-faith arguments will not be tolerated. Issues or discussions deviating into harassment or political polemics will be closed immediately.
