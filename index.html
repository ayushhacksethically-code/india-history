MANY CIVILIZATIONS · ONE LAND

From the Indus cities to the Deccan villages — India’s story has always been plural.

Step into nine millennia of settled life and five millennia of recorded history — a subcontinent where urban citadels, agrarian valleys, and forest communities evolved simultaneously and in constant exchange.

‘Nine millennia’ refers to settled village life from c. 7000 BCE onward. ‘Five millennia’ refers to the recorded civilizational narrative from the Mature Harappan phase (c. 2600 BCE). Both are approximations.

Central Editorial Thesis

The Oldest Truth About India

A historical retrospective of continuous civilizational diversity.

In one line: India was never a single uniform empire; multiple urban, farming, and forest communities always thrived together.

India was never one uniform civilization. At every period — including the Bronze Age — multiple urban, agrarian, pastoral and tribal civilizations existed side by side, and in constant interaction. This plurality-in-connection, not uniformity, is India’s oldest and most continuous feature.

While engineers built planned brick cities at Mohenjo-daro and Harappa in the northwest, stone tool users lived in pit dwellings in Kashmir, farmers along the Ganga grew rice, and pastoralists in southern India gathered around sacred ash mounds. Plurality is not a modern political invention; it has been the living reality of the subcontinent since the very beginning.

India was plural before it was a nation.

Village Roots & Indus Urbanism c. 7000 – 1300 BCE Early farming at Mehrgarh and Bhirrana; mature orthogonal grid cities at Harappa alongside regional cultures.
